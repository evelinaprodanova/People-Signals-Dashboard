# Limits and blind spots

Read this before you use the tool on real data.

## What it is not

- **Not validated.** The signals and thresholds are reasoned, not tested against real exits. On the demo data they work because the data was built to show them.
- **Not a predictor.** A status is a prompt for a conversation. It does not say anyone will leave.
- **Not a verdict on people.** Never use it to judge a manager or a team. Manager scores are for offering support.
- **Not a compliance tool.** It cannot tell you whether your use is lawful. That depends on your country, your employees' notice, your purpose and your agreements.

## Where it can mislead

- **Small numbers are noisy.** One exit on a team of eight is 12.5% turnover. The page adds ranges, but it cannot make a small team's numbers solid.
- **Non-response is handled, not solved.** The adjustment is an assumption. A real analysis would also compare who answers with who does not, by tenure, location and level.
- **Two surveys, same people.** The same person answers both, which inflates the link between manager score and pulse.
- **Survey fatigue.** Two surveys every quarter ask a lot. If response falls, everything weakens.
- **Correlations across few teams.** One team can swing a result. The 95% ranges treat team-quarters as independent, so they are too narrow.
- **Compa-ratio is not fair pay.** Level, location, tenure and a recent promotion round all move it. A ratio above 1.05 means paid above the market midpoint on average. It does not mean paid fairly.
- **Pay is a snapshot.** It changes at review cycles, not quarterly.
- **Early signals are proxies.** Overtime, open roles and leave not taken stand in for workload. They miss unrecorded effort.
- **Early signals overlap.** A manager change often comes with a reorg and a heavy quarter. The groups are counted once each, but the overlap is reduced, not removed.
- **A gap between groups is not proof of unfair treatment.** Level, role and tenure can create a gap. A pooled figure can hide a gap inside a team or invent one from the mix of teams.
- **Items that move together.** A strong team scores well on every pulse item, so the driver ranking is weak evidence of what matters.
- **Cost is an assumption.** A flat share of a year's pay ignores role, seniority and how long a role stays empty.
- **Span is not quality.** A wide span can work for experienced people doing similar work.
- **Thresholds from the data are relative.** They always flag the top quarter of teams. They need absolute floors set from past exits.
- **The backtest is a first look.** Four quarters give two test windows.
- **Text analysis is a keyword match.** It misses sarcasm and other languages.

## What it does not cover

Regretted attrition beyond a count, internal moves, reorgs and layoffs on the timeline, hiring and onboarding data, key-person risk, business outcomes, external benchmarks, role-based access, shared action tracking, and connections to an HRIS or survey tool.

## Behaviour risks

- A team marked "Act" can be treated as a problem before anything is wrong.
- If managers learn that a score draws attention, answers drift toward the middle.
- The equity view shows sensitive cuts. Keep it with the people who own pay and fairness.

## Before you rely on it

1. Test each signal against past exits, hold some quarters aside, and drop the ones that do not hold up.
2. Agree purpose, access and retention with legal and employee representatives.
3. Start with one leader and one quarter, then ask whether the page changed what they did.
